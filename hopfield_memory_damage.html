Hebbian learning rule
W_ij = (1/N) Σ_μ ξ^μ_i · ξ^μ_j (i ≠ j, W_ii = 0)
Neurons that fire together, wire together. The weight matrix stores p patterns as associative memories. States are binary ±1 (Ising spins). This is equivalent to a symmetric spin glass with random couplings.
Asynchronous update rule
s_i(t+1) = sign( Σ_j W_ij · s_j(t) )
One neuron updated at a time, chosen at random. Equivalent to zero-temperature Glauber dynamics. The network always moves to lower energy — guaranteed by the Lyapunov function.
Energy function (Lyapunov)
E = −½ Σ_ij W_ij · s_i · s_j
E decreases monotonically under asynchronous updates. Stored memories are local minima. Spurious attractors are additional minima (spin-glass "metastable states"). The network is a content-addressable memory: inputs cue recall by starting in the basin of the correct minimum.
Storage capacity (Amit et al. 1985)
p_max ≈ 0.138 · N
Derived via replica method from statistical mechanics. Above this capacity, retrieval errors appear. The phase transition is analogous to the ferromagnet→spin glass transition in the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model. The "memory phase" (m → 1) gives way to a "confused phase" (m → 0) at the critical loading ratio α = p/N ≈ 0.138.
Overlap order parameter
m^μ = (1/N) Σ_i ξ^μ_i · s_i ∈ [−1, 1]
m = 1: perfect retrieval of pattern μ. m = −1: retrieved the inverted pattern. m ≈ 0: spurious attractor (no memory retrieved). In the thermodynamic limit, m satisfies a self-consistency equation m = tanh(βm) — the familiar mean-field magnetisation.
Damage models
Neuron lesion: s_i = 0 for i ∈ lesion set. Reduces effective N, shrinks capacity. Analogous to focal hippocampal damage (HM patient, stroke).

Weight decay: W → (1−α)W. Scales down all attractors equally. Analogous to Alzheimer's β-amyloid-mediated synaptic loss.

Synaptic noise: W_ij → W_ij + η_ij, η ~ N(0,σ²). Adds spurious connections. Analogous to diffuse axonal injury (TBI), schizophrenia-like disconnection.
